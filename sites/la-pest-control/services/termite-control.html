Termites Are a Real Risk in LA's Older Housing Stock

Los Angeles has two termite species that matter most to homeowners: subterranean termites, which build mud tubes from the soil into a structure, and drywood termites, which infest exposed wood directly — including attics, eaves, and furniture — without needing soil contact. Because so much of LA's housing was built decades ago, wood framing, older stucco, and aging rooflines create exactly the conditions both species look for.

Signs of a Termite Infestation

  • Mud tubes running along the foundation, crawlspace, or exterior walls
  • Discarded, translucent wings near windowsills or doors, especially after a warm day
  • Wood that sounds hollow when tapped, or that crumbles under light pressure
  • Small piles of frass (drywood termite droppings that look like fine sawdust or coffee grounds)
  • Sagging floors, door frames that stick, or bubbling paint on wood surfaces

Buying or Selling a Home?

Real estate transactions in California often require a Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) inspection report. We can provide a termite inspection ahead of escrow so you have documentation of the property's condition before closing.
