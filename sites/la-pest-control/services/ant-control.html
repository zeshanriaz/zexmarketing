Ants Are a Persistent Problem in LA Yards and Homes

Argentine ants dominate ant activity across Los Angeles, forming massive interconnected super-colonies that trail long distances in search of moisture and food. Odorous house ants are also common, especially around kitchens and bathrooms. Because these colonies can contain multiple queens spread across many nesting sites, a single can of spray rarely reaches the source of the problem — and it can even backfire by driving the colony to split and spread further.

Signs You Have an Ant Problem

  • Ant trails leading to food, water, or sweet spills
  • Increased indoor activity after rain or when the yard is watered
  • Small trails or hills along the foundation, sidewalks, or under mulch
  • Ants gathering around pet food bowls or trash areas
  • Visible trails along baseboards, countertops, or window sills

Our Ant Control Process

1

Inspection

We trace trails back toward nesting areas and identify the moisture sources that are drawing ants in.

2

Baiting Program

Slow-acting bait is carried back to the colony by worker ants, working through the full population rather than just the ants you can see.

3

Exterior Perimeter Treatment

A barrier treatment is applied around the foundation and known entry points to reduce new activity.

4

Moisture & Landscaping Guidance

We advise on trimming vegetation away from the structure and fixing irrigation overspray and other moisture sources that attract ants.

Why Spraying Ant Trails Doesn't Work

Contact sprays only kill the ants you can see and can trigger a process called "budding," where a stressed colony splits into multiple smaller colonies and spreads the problem to new areas of the property. Baiting takes a different approach, targeting the queen and the colony as a whole so the population is reduced at its source rather than just along the surface trail.

Frequently Asked Questions

Store-bought sprays kill only the ants they touch and can trigger "budding," where a stressed colony splits into several smaller colonies. This often makes the problem spread rather than go away.

Bait-based treatment typically shows a noticeable reduction in activity within a week or two as the colony works through the bait and the population declines.

Activity typically increases in spring and after rain, as colonies expand and search for water sources, which is when we see the most service calls for ants.
